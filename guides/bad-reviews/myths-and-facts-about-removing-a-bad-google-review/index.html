Myths and Facts About Removing a Bad Google Review

Google will take down a bad review of your business when that review violates Google's review rules, and in every other case it stays up. That single condition decides nearly everything about removal. It is also why so many owners come away frustrated: they flag a review that feels unfair, wait a few days, and find it still sitting on their profile. The rules were never written to referee disagreements between a company and its customers. They were written to catch abuse.

Rootstock Growth lays out those rules briefly in its section on removing a bad Google review. This piece covers the same ground through the beliefs owners tend to bring with them.

What the numbers say about which reviews can come down

Begin with what bad reviews of local service companies actually contain. In November 2026, Rootstock Growth read 852 one- and two-star Google reviews with text, every one of them left for the HVAC, plumbing, roofing or electrical companies Google ranked highest across Atlanta, Dallas, Memphis, Sebring and St. Louis. According to Rootstock Growth, 67% were mainly about how the customer was treated, 21% were about the work itself, and 12% were about both.

Picture what fills that 67%. A price surprise. A callback that never happened. A crew that rolled in late. These are firsthand accounts from people who actually hired the company. Under Google's policy, someone who had a poor experience and describes it politely is entitled to post it. That means the bulk of what pulls a rating down sits outside the reach of any report, however carefully it is filed.

Five beliefs about removal that don't hold up

Belief: if a review is unfair, Google will take it down. Fairness isn't the test. Google's policy says, "Do not report a review just because you disagree with it or dislike it," and Google adds that it "doesn't get involved in conflict between businesses and customers." Whether the customer's version of events matches yours is simply not a question Google settles.

Belief: nothing ever gets removed. Plenty does, once it crosses a line. Google's policy covers fake reviews and rating manipulation, reviews posted by competitors or by people with a conflict of interest, harassment, hate speech, profanity, personal information, and off-topic rants unconnected to any real visit. A one-star from somebody who never hired you, or a rival business sniping at you, gives you solid grounds to report.

Belief: reporting is complicated. It happens inside your Business Profile. Open the reviews, select Report beside the one in question, and choose the reason that fits it.

Belief: you'll hear back at once, and the decision is final. Neither holds. Google says review evaluation "typically takes several days," and the report can be followed in Google's Reviews Management Tool while you wait. If Google finds no violation, you are allowed a single appeal. Flag it, wait, appeal one time if needed, and the process is over.

Belief: paying a service to delete reviews is a smart shortcut. It is a risk to your own listing. Google's policy forbids offering anything in exchange for getting a negative review removed, which means a paid workaround can endanger the very profile you are trying to protect. Any removal-for-a-fee pitch deserves suspicion.

What removal can't fix

Removal is a narrow tool aimed at a narrow problem. It handles the fake, the abusive and the off-topic. It does nothing for the review where a homeowner explains that the final bill landed far above the quote, because that review is legitimate.

For those, two things carry the weight. The first is your reply, since future customers read it before they pick up the phone. Here the best-ranked companies set the pace: those in the top three on Google Maps answer 64% of their bad reviews, against 51% for everyone ranked below them, according to Rootstock Growth. The second is the process behind the complaint. Price surprises, silence and lost time are things a business can actually change, and changing them means fewer of these reviews get written at all.
